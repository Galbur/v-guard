Brand Idea

02 · Foundation
Quality over speed.

A small, serious automotive studio. More time goes into preparing the car and laying the film, so the result is cleaner and lasts longer. The identity should make a car owner think: “These people will work carefully on my car.”

Professional

Direction: premium automotive workshop + modern European design. Not ultra-luxury concierge, not racing, not street tuning.

Safe Space and Minimum Size

06 · Logo
X X X X
Clear space
X = ¼ of the shield height on every side. Nothing else — text, photo edges, other logos — enters this zone.
Minimum size
Mark only: 24 px digital / 8 mm print.
Horizontal lockup: 120 px / 30 mm wide.
Stacked lockup: 72 px / 20 mm wide.
Placement
Top-left or centred. On photos, prefer the mark in a corner over a full lockup across the image.

Any colour outside the palette

Photography Style

14 · Photo
Light — controlled, soft, one direction. Dark or neutral surroundings. No coloured LED light.
Contrast — high but real. Blacks stay black, gold in the frame only if it is the logo.
Subject — show the work, not just the car: edges, seams, prep, hands, finished panels.
Avoid — wide-angle distortion, HDR, heavy vignettes, wet-look street shots, people posing with the car.

Brand Voice

27 · Copy
Write like this
“Protect your paint. Keep the original look.”
“Professional PPF installation with careful surface preparation.”
“Tell us what you drive and what you want to protect — we’ll recommend the right option.”
“A full front takes 3–4 days. That time goes into preparation.”
Professional, direct, calm. Written for a car owner who is not an expert. Short sentences. Numbers where they help.
Not like this
“Transform your driving experience with unparalleled excellence.”
“Best PPF studio in the Netherlands. #1 in quality.”
“Flawless, perfect results every time.”
“Look at the result! 🔥🔥🔥”
Banned: best, #1, industry leader, flawless, perfect, highest quality in Europe, unparalleled. Confident claims must be checkable: film spec, warranty years, days in studio.

What Supports and What Breaks the Brand

28 · Summary
Supports
Black surfaces, gold as a thin accent, lots of negative space
Real photos of the work: edges, prep, hands, finished panels
One typeface, sentence-case headlines, tracked uppercase labels
Facts: film spec, days in studio, warranty, “from” prices
Calm copy that a non-expert understands
Breaks
Gold gradients, chrome, glow, gold-filled sections
Flames, flags, speed lines, carbon texture, car silhouettes
Coloured text boxes, emoji and country flags on video
“Best”, “#1”, “flawless”, countdowns, strike-through prices
Aggressive angles, HDR, wet-street cinematic shots of every car
